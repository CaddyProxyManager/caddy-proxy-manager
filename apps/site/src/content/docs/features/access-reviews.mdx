---
title: Access reviews
description: Have reviewers confirm or revoke who holds what - roles, group memberships, grants, API tokens and SCIM connections.
---

Access piles up. Someone changes teams and keeps the Operator role, a token made for a migration
is still valid a year later, a group still manages a host nobody in it looks after. An **access
review** asks the people who know, item by item, whether each piece of access should stay, and then
does what they decided.

Reviews live under **Users → Access reviews**.

## Starting a review

**Start** asks for a name, what to review, a due date and the reviewers. What to review is one of:

| Scope | What it asks about |
| --- | --- |
| Every account | Each active account's role, and each of its group memberships |
| Holders of a role | Each active account with that role, and each member of a group that [carries it](../users-and-groups/) |
| One group | The group's members, and each of its grants on a host or agent |
| Host and agent grants | Every group's grant on a proxy host, L4 host or agent |
| Every API token | Every [API token](../api/) that still works, with its owner |
| Every SCIM connection | Every identity provider allowed to [provision accounts](../scim-provisioning/) |

Starting a review takes a snapshot: each item keeps the names it had then, so a review still reads
after an account, group or token is gone. Disabled accounts and expired tokens open nothing and are
left out.

The items are shared among the reviewers. Everything about one person goes to one reviewer, and
**nobody is asked about their own access**: an item whose only reviewer is its subject goes to
whoever started the review, and is left unassigned if that is the same person. Anyone who manages
users can **Reassign** an item to someone else while the review is open.

### Hints

Some items carry a hint, worked out when the review starts:

- **No sign-in in 90 days** - the account has not completed a sign-in for 90 days, or never has and
  is older than that.
- **Token unused in 90 days** - the API token has not been used for 90 days, or never has.
- **No provisioning in 90 days** - the SCIM connection has not been used for 90 days.
- **Group has no members** - a grant on a group nobody is in.

## Deciding

A reviewer needs no permission of their own. Anyone with items waiting sees a banner on every page,
with the earliest due date, and **Review** takes them to the list. A reviewer sees only the reviews
they have items in, and only their own items within them.

Each item gets one of:

- **Keep it** - nothing changes.
- **Revoke it** - for a role, the account is **disabled**, which signs it out and stops its API
  tokens; a membership is removed; a grant is removed; a token is deleted; a SCIM connection is
  switched off (the accounts it provisioned stay as they are).
- **Change it** - a role becomes another role, or a grant moves between view and manage. To keep an
  account with less access, change its role rather than revoking it.

A note can go with any decision. Decisions can be changed until the review closes, and every one is
recorded in the [audit log](../audit-log/) with its item, the decision and the note.

### Accounts and groups provisioned by SCIM

An identity provider that [provisions accounts over SCIM](../scim-provisioning/) owns them: it
would re-enable an account disabled here on its next update, and put back a membership of a group
it provisioned. So **revoking is refused** for an account a SCIM connection provisioned and for a
membership of a SCIM group; the item says **Provisioned by SCIM**. Remove that access in the
identity provider instead. Keeping it, or changing the account's role (which is CPM's own), still
works.

## Closing

Someone who manages users **Closes** the review when it is done; it can be closed before its due
date. Reviewing ends, and items nobody decided stay as they are.

The revocations and changes are then **applied as the person closing it**, through exactly the
checks they would meet doing each by hand:

- disabling an account or changing its role needs permission to change users, and only for an
  account holding no more than they do; the new role must be one they could hand out;
- removing a membership or grant needs permission to change groups, and changing a grant means
  managing every host or agent the group's grants name;
- deleting someone else's token needs permission to change API tokens;
- switching off a SCIM connection needs permission to change both users and groups;
- nobody disables their own account, and the last active administrator is never disabled.

So a review can never do what its closer could not. An item that fails is marked **Not applied**
with the reason, the review waits as **Awaiting confirmation**, and **Confirm** tries the failures
again as whoever presses it, so an administrator can finish what a narrower role could not.
Something already gone (a token deleted meanwhile) is marked **Already gone**.

### Waiting for confirmation

With **Settings → Authentication → Access reviews → Revocations wait for confirmation** on
(`ACCESS_REVIEW_CONFIRM_REVOCATIONS`, off by default), closing applies nothing: the review waits as
**Awaiting confirmation** until someone who manages users presses **Confirm**. Use it when the
person closing reviews should not be the one carrying out revocations.

## Reminders

Reminders go through [alerting](../alerts/) as the **Access reviews** notification
(`NOTIFY_ACCESS_REVIEWS`, on by default), to the administrators by email and browser notification
and to any alert rule's channels that name them:

| Event | When |
| --- | --- |
| Access review due | A number of days before the due date (**Access review reminder**, `ACCESS_REVIEW_REMINDER_DAYS`, 3 by default), while items are undecided |
| Access review overdue | The due date passed with items still undecided |
| Access review waits for confirmation | A review closed with revocations held for confirmation |

Each is sent once per review. A review is due at the end of its due date, in UTC.

## Exporting

**Export** downloads a review as CSV, one row per item: what it was, who reviewed it, the decision
and note, when it was decided, and whether it was applied (or why not). Kinds, decisions and
outcomes are written as codes (`role`, `revoke`, `applied`), so a spreadsheet filter works whatever
language the file was exported in. An export is recorded in the audit log, since it lists who holds
what.

## Permissions

| Who | May |
| --- | --- |
| Users at **Read** or more | See every review and every item, and export them |
| Users at **Read and change** | Start, rename, reassign, close, confirm and delete reviews |
| A reviewer | See and decide their own items, whatever their role |

Deleting a review deletes its decisions; what it already revoked stays revoked.

## GraphQL

`accessReviews` and `accessReview(id)` list what the caller may see, as above.
`createAccessReview`, `updateAccessReview`, `reassignAccessReviewItems` and `deleteAccessReview`
need permission to change users; `decideAccessReviewItem` takes only the caller's own items; and
`closeAccessReview` and `confirmAccessReview`, which revoke more than any one token area covers,
need a full-scope token.
