The **Alerts** page holds three things: **rules** that decide what is worth telling, **channels**
that carry it, and the **history** of what was raised and how each delivery went. A fourth tab sets
up **daily digests**.

Nothing changes on upgrade. Every kind of [notification](/features/email/#notifications) is a
built-in rule routed to email and browser push, and its on switch stays where it was, under
**Settings → Email → Notifications**, with each administrator's own choices under **Profile →
Notifications**. Until someone edits a rule, the same people get the same emails and pushes.

## Channels

Email and push are built in: email goes to the Recipients list or the administrators, push to each
administrator's subscribed browsers. Add any number of these:

| Channel | What it posts | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Webhook | JSON, signed as below | Custom headers; plain http only to an address on your own network |
| Discord | Native embeds, coloured by severity | Posted with `?wait=true`, so a message Discord drops is an error, not silence |
| Slack | An incoming-webhook message: `text` plus blocks | `text` is always present, as the notification and the fallback |
| Microsoft Teams | An Adaptive Card | The URL of a Workflows flow started by "When a Teams webhook request is received" |
| ntfy | JSON published to the server root | Server, topic and an optional access token |

Teams' old Office 365 connector webhooks were shut down in 2026 and are not offered. A Workflows
flow can accept a request before the card is posted, so **Test** on a Teams channel says the post
was accepted, not that it was delivered.

**Test** posts straight to the channel, saved or as typed in the form. Webhook URLs, signing
secrets, header values and ntfy tokens are stored encrypted and never shown again; the list shows a
channel's address without its path. Leave a secret field empty when editing to keep the stored one.

### Signed webhooks

Webhook requests follow the Standard Webhooks scheme, so any of its verifiers works unchanged:

- `webhook-id`: the message id. It is the same on every retry of the same alerts, so **drop an id
  you have already seen** to ignore a replay.
- `webhook-timestamp`: seconds since the epoch. **Refuse one more than five minutes from your
  clock.** It is inside the signature, so it cannot be changed without the check failing.
- `webhook-signature`: `v1,` and the base64 HMAC-SHA256 of `id.timestamp.body`, keyed with the
  bytes after `whsec_` in the signing secret, base64-decoded.

Leave the signing secret empty on a new channel and one is made for you and shown once; copy it then.
The body is `{ "type": "alerts", "timestamp": ..., "data": { "subject", "url", "alerts": [...] } }`,
each alert with its kind, severity, title, sentence, time and the event's own fields. A digest
arrives with `"type": "digest"`.

### Batching, retries and failing channels

Each channel sends at most one message a minute: whatever is raised within it goes together, as
email always has. Discord takes up to 10 alerts a message, Slack, Teams and ntfy a handful more;
the rest follow in the next. A problem is told once while it lasts and its recovery once, and one
that is over before it was sent costs nothing.

A failed send is retried after 30 seconds, then a minute, doubling to an hour. A service asking to
slow down is obeyed: Discord's `retry_after` (to the millisecond) and Slack's and Teams'
`Retry-After` are waited out when short and become the retry time when long. Alerts a day old are
dropped rather than sent late. The built-in email keeps its five-minute retry.

Three failures in a row make the channel itself a problem, told on your other channels (never on
the failing one) through the built-in **Alert channel failing** rule, and resolved when it works
again.

## Rules

A rule has a **source**, a **scope**, a **severity**, its **channels**, an optional **quiet period**
and can be **silenced** until a time.

| Source | What it watches |
| --- | --- |
| Events | The notification events: an agent offline, upstream errors, a refused configuration, a failing backup and the rest |
| Needs attention | Any item [Needs attention](/features/overview/) lists, such as a certificate close to expiry or an LDAP directory unreachable |
| Traffic signal | 5xx bursts, mitigation spikes and blocked-traffic concentrations from [analytics](/features/analytics/) |
| Metric | A threshold per proxy host over the last N minutes, read from ClickHouse: 5xx share (percent), requests, 5xx responses or mitigated requests, above or below a value |

Rules on Needs attention, signals and metrics are checked once a minute. Each thing a rule finds
(one host over its threshold, one burst) is raised once and resolved when it is gone. A source
that could not be read resolves nothing, and metric rules wait while analytics are off.

- **Scope**: all hosts, chosen proxy hosts, or hosts with any of some [tags](/features/reverse-proxy/).
  A scoped rule only matches what names a host.
- **Quiet period**: after telling, the rule stays quiet about the same thing for this long, so a
  host that flaps is told once.
- **Silence**: nothing is raised until the time you pick.

A built-in rule keeps its source; its channels, severity, quiet period and silence can be changed,
and its on switch stays the Settings toggle. **Test** on a rule queues a test alert through its
channels, as anything it raises would go.

## History

Every alert is kept for 90 days with each channel's delivery: sent, waiting (with attempts and the
last error), failed, dropped (its rule turned off, or a day old), or withdrawn (over before it was
sent). Filter by rule, channel, severity, type or delivery status.

## Daily digest

A digest is sent every day at a time you pick, in a time zone you pick, to email and any added
channel. It covers the 24 hours before it is sent:

- traffic and mitigations by outcome, the most attacked hosts and paths, and the most triggered WAF
  rules;
- countries and networks (ASNs) seen that were not seen in the 30 days before;
- certificates expiring within 14 days;
- configuration changes from the audit log;
- each scheduled backup's last run;
- what Needs attention lists.

Without analytics the traffic sections say so and the rest is sent as usual. Email goes to each
recipient in their own time zone (their profile's, or the digest's) and language; chat channels get
a compact form cut to fit Discord's and Teams' limits. **Preview** shows what it would say now and
**Send** sends it at once.

## More than one controller

Alerts are sent by the controller running the [background jobs](/features/high-availability/).
Every send claims its rows first, and every digest claims its day, so a change of controller mid
send neither drops nor doubles one. A digest due while no controller was running is sent once when
one takes over.

## API

GraphQL has `alertChannels`, `alertRules`, `alertHistory`, `alertDigests`, `alertDigestRuns` and
`previewAlertDigest`, with mutations to create, update, delete and test each. They need an
administrator, and a token scoped to settings.
