---
title: SCIM provisioning
description: Let an identity provider create, update and disable accounts and groups through SCIM 2.0.
---

An identity provider that speaks SCIM 2.0 can keep CPM's accounts and groups in step with its own
directory: people it assigns to CPM get an account, people it removes are disabled straight away,
and its groups arrive as CPM groups with the role you map them to.

:::caution[PostgreSQL only]
SCIM provisioning needs CPM to run on PostgreSQL. Under SQLite the SCIM endpoint answers
`501 Not Implemented` and the **Provisioning** page says so: SQLite is meant for trying CPM out, not
for an identity provider writing to it all day.
:::

## Connections

Each identity provider gets its own **connection**, made under **Users → Provisioning**. A
connection is a bearer token for SCIM and nothing else:

- A SCIM token is refused by the REST and GraphQL APIs, and an [API token](../api/) is refused by
  the SCIM endpoint.
- The token is shown once, when the connection is made or its token rotated. CPM keeps only a hash
  of it and shows its last four characters to tell connections apart.
- **Rotate** replaces the token at once; the old one stops working, so paste the new one into the
  identity provider straight after.
- Turning a connection **off** refuses its token until it is turned back on.
- Making, changing, rotating or deleting a connection needs permission to change both users and
  groups, since its token creates the first and fills the second. Over GraphQL these mutations need
  a full-scope token.

Give the identity provider the **SCIM base URL** the page shows, which ends in `/api/auth/scim/v2`,
and the token as a bearer token ("OAuth bearer token" in most identity providers).

## Accounts

- A provisioned person gets an account with the **User** role. What they may do comes from the
  groups they are in (below) or from an administrator.
- **Link existing accounts** (on by default): when someone is provisioned with the email of an
  account that already exists, the connection takes that account over instead of refusing the
  request. It keeps its name, role and sign-in methods; from then on the identity provider
  decides whether it is active. Turn this off to have such a request refused instead.
- **`active: false`**, which is how most identity providers deprovision, disables the account and,
  in the same transaction, ends its sessions and forward-auth sessions and deletes its API tokens.
  `"False"` as a string and a capitalised `"Replace"` operation are accepted too.
- **Deleting** a user through SCIM disables the account in the same way. It is not erased, so its
  history stays readable and an administrator decides whether it goes.
- The last active administrator cannot be disabled through SCIM; the request is refused and nothing
  changes.

People sign in the way they already do, usually through [single sign-on](../users-and-groups/#oauth--sso).
A provisioned account has no password; turn on **Auto-link accounts** on the OIDC provider (or list
the email domains on a SAML provider) so the first sign-in joins the provisioned account.

## Groups and roles

Each group the identity provider pushes becomes a CPM group with the source **SCIM**, and its
members follow the identity provider's membership. Anyone added to it on the Groups page stays.

Under **Group roles** a connection maps group names to roles, the same way an OIDC provider's group
claims do: a provisioned group whose name is listed under a role [gives that role](../users-and-groups/#a-role-carried-by-a-group)
to its members. Changing the mapping reaches existing groups at once. Groups cannot give the
administrator role, and you can only map roles you could hand out yourself.

A group name that already belongs to a group made on the Groups page is refused with a `409`, and
nothing from that request is kept.

## Deleting a connection

The token stops working, its groups move to the Groups page (source **UI**, members and all), and
its accounts are signed out but stay enabled, for an administrator to keep or remove.

## What CPM supports

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Resources | `Users` and `Groups`: create, replace, `PATCH` (add, replace, remove, with value filters such as `emails[type eq "work"].value`) and delete |
| Filters | `eq`, joined by `and`, on `userName`, `externalId` and the other attributes the Schemas endpoint lists; any other operator answers `400` `invalidFilter` |
| Paging | `startIndex` and `count` |
| Attribute selection | `attributes` and `excludedAttributes` |
| Discovery | `ServiceProviderConfig`, `Schemas` and `ResourceTypes` |
| Not supported | Bulk, `/Me` (both answer `501`), sorting, ETags, password changes and nested groups, each advertised as such |

Errors use SCIM's error schema with a `scimType` where one applies (`invalidFilter`,
`invalidPath`, `invalidValue`, `mutability`, `noTarget`, `uniqueness`), and responses are
`application/scim+json`. Every change a connection makes is in the [audit log](../audit-log/),
named with its connection.
